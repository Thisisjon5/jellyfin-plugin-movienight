## 1b. Zero-viewer feeder fix — `DONE (v0.3.31, verified live 2026-08-16 night)`

Verified against the done-criterion same night: last-consumer disconnect
killed the feeders ("last feed consumer gone" log), a 4.5-min zero-viewer
gap, then retune respawned the feeder at 674.0s (wall-clock-exact live
edge) with the first heartbeat at 4231 kbps — no catch-up burst (old
behavior was 12721 kbps). Position clock keeps running while nobody is
tuned (live-channel semantics).

Found in the 2026-08-16 mini-soak (W13 S3E7): when the last viewer
disconnects, the feeder blocks on its full stdout pipe while wall-clock
position keeps advancing; on the next tune, -re "catch-up" bursts ~3x
realtime (heartbeat evidence: 12.7 Mbps) and the rejoining client's playhead
lands the whole gap-length behind live - every admin action then reaches
screens that many minutes late (measured: stable ~1:50 echo for a ~2min
gap). Fix: when the feed.ts consumer disconnects, kill the feeder and mark
the session feeder-pending at the current wall position; the existing
lazy-start respawns it at the live edge on the next consumer.

**Done when:** tune → untune ~2min → retune shows the live position, no
bitrate burst in the heartbeats, and a pause reaches the rejoined client in
normal (~15-30s) client-buffer time.

## 1c. Android app / DirectPlay investigation — `open (found 2026-08-16 night); soak does NOT block on it`

Investigation results from the late-night web-broken scare (full trail:
ITERATION-LOG arc 8 addendum):

- **Server + channel exonerated.** Firefox (jellyfin-web), Xbox, and Roku
  all play the channel. The evening's desktop-Chrome "wedges" were an
  artifact of automated browser-tab testing (throttled/background tabs) —
  NOT a real bug. Manual testing by a human is the only valid client test
  for this channel.
- **Genuine failure: Jellyfin Android app (integrated player) + Live TV.**
  Player opens, black screen, playbar 00:00/00:00, on the FIRST tune -
  before any pause seam exists. Connectivity fine (VOD DirectPlays
  perfectly on the same phone). Hypothesis: the app DirectPlays the raw
  infinite TS and ExoPlayer chokes on its shape (endless, no index,
  mid-stream join) - every working client sits behind a server-side ffmpeg
  hop instead. Untested phone-side experiments: integrated player OFF
  (falls back to proven web player), or home quality cap below 8 Mbps
  (forces transcode path).
- **Server-side fix candidate (step-3 detail):** stop advertising
  DirectPlay for this channel so every client negotiates the remux/
  transcode hop. Library VOD DirectPlay untouched.
- **Timestamp continuity probe (cheap, do before ruling on DirectPlay):**
  measure whether the persistent remuxer's output is ALREADY continuous
  across a movie↔slate swap (v0.3.28's smoothing may happen in our remuxer
  or in Jellyfin's downstream hop - never measured which). If continuous,
  DirectPlay is safe-in-principle and Android is purely an ExoPlayer
  format question; if not, ffmpeg's setts bitstream filter can force
  continuity in copy mode.

**Live-edge model (settled, applies to all future work):** clients cannot
be commanded (spike 2, v0.3.4 - SendPlaystateCommand not honored on Live
TV) and always play the stream as delivered from its start. Therefore the
server must never possess/serve content behind the live edge - which is
what v0.3.31 guarantees. Residual: a client that rebuffers N seconds stays
N seconds behind forever (retune = only reset); pause still reaches it,
offset by its lag. Acceptable for movie night.

## 2. Movie-length soak — `FAILED 2026-08-18 (ran a day early); root-caused`

Jon hosts a real movie night through that page: full-length feature, a few
pauses spread across it, Web + Roku.

**Done when:** movie completes end-to-end with pause/resume cycles, sessions
survive throughout, logs clean, no orphan processes.

**OUTCOME (2026-08-18): FAILED.** The Creator, three clients (Xbox + Roku +
web), all froze together. Root cause measured: Jellyfin spawns one ffmpeg per
client for Live TV — always, even for byte-identical codecs — with
`-hls_playlist_type event -hls_list_size 0`, so every client accumulates the
whole broadcast on disk. O(N) writes to one 7200rpm spindle starved the feeder
(all three client remuxes ran at ~0.92x realtime). Full evidence:
ITERATION-LOG arc 9. This also answers the ride-along below: the Roku
"periodic stutter" from arc 6 was this same contention at N=2.

**Consequence: steps 3 and 4 below are superseded for the delivery layer.** The
new direction is `planning/DESIGN-abr-ladder.md` (shared ABR ladder over a
custom `ITunerHost`), ruled by Jon 2026-08-18. Its gating spike must run before
anything is built. The feeder/slate/pause half of v3 is unaffected and stays.

Rides along: Roku periodic-stutter investigation (arc 6 open finding b) —
diagnose from FFmpeg.Remux/DirectStream logs, don't guess. Slate warm-start
polish (finding a) slots here or in step 3, whichever the soak shows is
needed.

## 5. Captions spike: CC1 embedding vs burn-in — `backlog (Jon, 2026-08-16)`

Today the feeder strips all subtitle tracks - clients have no CC option.
Two candidate designs, in preference order:

- **CC1/EIA-608 embedding** (preferred if viable): captions live in H.264
  SEI side-data, so they survive the -c copy remuxer and Jellyfin's remux
  untouched, and viewers keep a per-client CC toggle (the broadcast-TV
  model). ffmpeg can pass A53 data through but cannot author it from an
  SRT - needs an external injection stage (libcaption or similar). Spike
  question 1, cheap: does A53 survive our exact pipeline (h264_qsv →
  copy remux → Jellyfin remux → Roku with CC on)? One encode-probe with an
  A53-bearing sample + one tuned client answers it, and gates everything
  else.
- **Burn-in fallback**: subtitles filter in the feeder before hwupload,
  host picks a track at Go Live. One evening of work, works on every
  client, but all-or-nothing for the whole room.

**Done when:** the spike verdict is recorded (CC1 viable or not) and
whichever design won is ruled by Jon for a build slot.
